High-dose vitamin C in cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised and non-randomised evidence

Raphael Malikian — Independent Researcher, Palmdale, CA, USA · rtmalikian@gmail.com · ORCID 0009-0008-4326-4205

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24213677 (Zenodo, CC BY 4.0) · Passed multi-agent pre-submission peer review, Gates 0–6, before deposit. High-dose vitamin C is not FDA-approved for cancer; this review makes no clinical recommendation.

⬇ Download the full paper (PDF) Data & code on GitHub
What the randomised evidence shows, by cancer type (survival hazard ratios): colorectal 1.04 (phase III, 442 patients) · pancreatic 0.46 (34 patients, stopped early) · lung 0.33 (vitamin C given with heat treatment) · prostate 1.98. The two trials at low risk of bias pool to HR 1.26. The four-trial pooled estimate (HR 0.73, Hartung–Knapp 95% CI 0.21–2.60, I² 87.5%) was judged uninterpretable as a common effect; the headline is trial-level. Read the full paper below.
